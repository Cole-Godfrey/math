# Remaining work

The objective remains a universal, living repository covering mathematical axioms, definitions, objects, theorems, and proofs through the research frontier, with complete machine-checked derivations and collaborative expansion.

The current implementation supplies a seed library and registered mathlib modules, transitive dependency extraction, explicit axiom policy, shared expression storage, a local browser explorer with immutable query snapshots, subject browsing, complete dependency hierarchies, replayable proof downloads, archived source attribution, and contribution checks. Mathematical coverage includes number systems through complex numbers, cardinality, Sylow theorems, rank-nullity, commutative algebra, finite and infinite Galois correspondence, normal bases, the Nullstellensatz, topology, the fundamental theorems of algebra and calculus, measure theory, Pythagoras, extremal graph theory, categories, discrete probability, recursive functions, halting undecidability, oracle reductions, and Turing machine simulations. Lean and nanoda verification are required for publication. See [representative results](coverage.md).

Every indexed logical declaration is bound to the exact proof export accepted by both checkers. Lossless name identities, reduction hints, definition groups, and recursor rules are retained and compared; packed fingerprints and shared immutable universe requirements limit verification overhead. Catalog reading streams records and shares repeated JSON field names while retaining every value.

Number theory now includes modular arithmetic, quadratic reciprocity, Pell equations, Dedekind ideal factorization, finite ideal class groups, Minkowski bounds, Dirichlet units, regulators, the product formula, and the Kummer-Dedekind criterion.

Browser and terminal queries share immutable query snapshots. Terminal search, dependency, and export commands retain complete results while avoiding the full proof expression array. Catalog-only terminal use remains available when a query receipt was not retained.

The browser inspects exact term occurrences with their binder contexts, ordered application arguments, local definitions, referenced declarations, and recursor reduction rules. Structured navigation reads each requested path from the immutable snapshot and retains full term downloads.

Natural literals retain every decimal digit beyond Python's default conversion limit, including in proof replay, stored expressions, and browser inspection. Catalog tests release the earlier expression arrays before loading the complete corpus again.

Pinned upstream bibliographies and compiled module documentation connect explicit citation keys to retained records, publication links, and source locations. The browser distinguishes declaration citations from module references and preserves the complete bibliography for reconstruction.

Probability coverage includes laws and distributions, independence and conditional independence, conditional expectations, Borel-Cantelli lemmas, the strong laws of large numbers, the real central limit theorem, stopping times, optional sampling, and martingale convergence. Statement checks retain the precise integrability, independence, boundedness, and convergence hypotheses.

Algebraic topology includes paths, fundamental groupoids and groups, higher homotopy groups, contractible and simply connected spaces, coverings, circle winding numbers, singular simplicial sets, geometric realization, chain complexes, and singular homology. Signature checks cover the circle's fundamental group, commutativity of higher homotopy groups, homotopy invariance, and zeroth homology as a coproduct over path components.

First-order logic and model theory include syntax, realization, prenex forms, elementary embeddings and substructures, ultraproducts, compactness, Löwenheim–Skolem theorems, definability, complete types and their topology, Fraïssé limits, and theories of orders, graphs, rings, and fields. Statement checks include completeness of algebraically closed fields, the Lefschetz principle, Presburger semilinearity and periodicity, and undefinability of multiplication.

Set theory includes all modules in the pinned library's `SetTheory` directory: ZFC sets and classes, ranks and the cumulative hierarchy, ordinals and cardinal numbers, arithmetic, Cantor normal forms, constructive ordinal notation, Veblen functions, aleph and beth hierarchies, cofinality, regular cardinals, club and stationary sets, finite hereditary lists, and prefix-closed trees. Signature checks cover the set-theoretic laws, the Burali–Forti paradox, ordinal representation, the hierarchy's cardinalities, normal notation arithmetic, and Fodor's lemma.

Local proofs connect finite ordinal notations with epsilon zero in the Veblen hierarchy. They establish the bound for all raw notations, a unique normal representation, and an order isomorphism with every ordinal below epsilon zero, together with arithmetic compatibility and exponentiation closure.

Differential equations and dynamical systems include all modules in the pinned library’s `Analysis/ODE` and `Dynamics` directories. Coverage includes integral curves, Picard–Lindelöf existence and local flows, ODE uniqueness, Grönwall inequalities, fixed and periodic points, omega-limit sets, minimal and transitive actions, Poincaré recurrence, invariant probability measures, ergodic circle maps, translation numbers, topological entropy, symbolic subshifts, and algebraic Newton iteration. Signature checks retain the assumptions and domains of the representative results.

Functional analysis includes all modules in the pinned library’s `Analysis/InnerProductSpace`, `Analysis/Normed/Module`, `Analysis/Normed/Operator`, `Analysis/Normed/Algebra`, and `Analysis/LocallyConvex` directories. Coverage includes Hilbert spaces and projections, duality, Hahn–Banach extension and separation, uniform boundedness, open mapping and closed graphs, Banach–Alaoglu, compact and Fredholm operators, spectral theory, singular values, mean ergodic convergence, tensor seminorms, reproducing kernels, Banach algebras, and Gelfand–Mazur. Local Lax–Milgram proofs give the unique weak solution for every continuous linear load, package the solution map as a continuous linear equivalence, and prove quantitative bounds on the solution and its response to perturbations.

Harmonic analysis includes all modules in the pinned library’s `Analysis/Fourier`, `Analysis/Distribution`, and `Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Gaussian` directories. Coverage includes Fourier inversion, Plancherel, convolution, circle Fourier series and Parseval, Poisson summation, finite character duality, Gaussian transforms and sums, test functions, distributions, Schwartz spaces, tempered distributions, Fourier multipliers, distributional support, and Bessel-potential Sobolev membership. Local proofs give a unique tempered solution to the normalized elliptic equation `u - ((2π)²)⁻¹ Δu = f`, identify its Fourier multiplier, and prove a gain of two Sobolev orders for every exponent at least one.

Complex analysis includes every module in the pinned library’s `Analysis/Complex`, `Analysis/Analytic`, and `Analysis/Meromorphic` directories. Coverage includes power series and their radii, analytic inversion, isolated zeros and orders, the identity principle, Cauchy integrals and estimates, Taylor expansions, maximum modulus, Schwarz and Hadamard bounds, open mapping, removable singularities, harmonic conjugates, Poisson formulas, Jensen’s formula, zero and pole divisors, the first main theorem, Gamma meromorphicity, complex polynomials, and the hyperbolic upper half-plane. Local proofs classify entire Banach-valued maps with bounded derivative as uniquely affine, determine their distances and boundedness, and prove scalar bijectivity for nonzero slope. A local extension of the harmonic Poisson formula to real Banach targets proves boundary representation and uniqueness on closed discs. Riemann mapping and the second main theorem have preparatory lemmas at this revision; their complete statements remain work to integrate.

Local harmonic principles now include sharp two-sided Harnack inequalities on open discs, uniform bounds on smaller closed discs, interior-zero and positivity results, strong minimum and maximum principles on connected open domains, and Liouville theorems for nonnegative or one-sided bounded entire harmonic functions. Their proofs use the retained Poisson representation, kernel bounds, circle averages, continuity, and explicit connectedness arguments. Banach-valued harmonic maps also satisfy boundary norm bounds and a distance bound for perturbations of boundary values. The proof uses positivity and unit mass of the Poisson kernel.
